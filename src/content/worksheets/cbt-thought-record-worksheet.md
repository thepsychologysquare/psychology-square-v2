Negative thoughts feel true in the moment — that's exactly what makes them hard to challenge without a structured process. This CBT thought record worksheet walks you through the same evidence-examination technique used in cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) sessions, so you can slow down an automatic thought and test whether it actually holds up.

Based on the classic Beck cognitive model, this worksheet guides you through seven steps: identifying the triggering situation, naming your emotion and rating its intensity, capturing the exact automatic thought, weighing evidence for and against it, and arriving at a more balanced, accurate thought. You'll finish by re-rating your emotional intensity to see the shift for yourself.

### Who this worksheet is for
- Anyone experiencing anxiety, depression, or persistent negative self-talk
- Therapy clients doing CBT homework between sessions
- Individuals practicing self-guided cognitive restructuring

### How to use it
Complete a new thought record each time you notice a strong emotional reaction — the sooner after the moment, the more accurate your recall of the automatic thought will be. With repeated use, patterns in your thinking begin to surface, which makes future entries faster and more insightful.
