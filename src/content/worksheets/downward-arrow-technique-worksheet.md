Surface-level negative thoughts are often symptoms of something deeper. The downward arrow technique is a core CBT tool used to trace an automatic thought back to the underlying core belief driving it — beliefs about worth, safety, or lovability that often go unexamined for years.

This worksheet guides you step by step: starting with one automatic thought, you repeatedly ask "If that's true, what would it mean?" until the answers converge on a stable core belief. From there, you'll reflect on when the belief first took shape and identify current evidence that contradicts it.

### Who this worksheet is for
- Therapy clients working on deeper cognitive restructuring
- Individuals who feel "stuck" using surface-level thought records alone
- Anyone wanting to understand the origin of recurring negative self-beliefs

### How to use it
This exercise can bring up real emotion, since it targets beliefs formed early in life — that's a normal part of the process, not a sign you're doing it wrong. Best used with a therapist's guidance, or as a self-reflection tool once you're familiar with basic thought-record work.
