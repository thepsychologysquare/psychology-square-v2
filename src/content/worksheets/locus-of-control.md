# Locus of Control Worksheet

The **Locus of Control Worksheet** from The Psychology Square is a structured psychology resource designed to help individuals develop greater awareness of how they understand responsibility, influence, and control in challenging situations. When something goes wrong, it can be easy to place responsibility entirely on ourselves or, alternatively, attribute the outcome primarily to circumstances outside our control. This worksheet provides a framework for examining these influences more objectively.

## What Is Locus of Control?

**Locus of control** refers to the way individuals perceive the relationship between their own actions and the outcomes they experience. In everyday life, people may differ in how much influence they believe they have over particular situations.

The concept can be useful when examining personal responsibility, decision-making, self-awareness, and responses to difficult circumstances. The worksheet specifically encourages consideration of both personal and environmental influences rather than automatically assigning responsibility to one side.

This makes it a potentially useful resource for exploring how a person interprets a specific problem, behavior, or challenging situation.

## Internal and External Influences

Situations are rarely determined by a single factor. Personal characteristics, actions, choices, circumstances, other people, and environmental conditions can all influence what happens.

The **Locus of Control Worksheet** provides a structured way to consider these different influences and examine the degree of control associated with them. This can encourage more nuanced thinking about responsibility and influence rather than relying on an overly simple interpretation of events.

The purpose is not to assume that every situation is completely within your control. Instead, the exercise can help distinguish between factors that may be influenced and factors over which a person has limited or no control.

Developing awareness of perceived control can be relevant to **personal growth, problem-solving, decision-making, behavioral change, and psychological self-awareness**. Recognizing what can and cannot be influenced may help an individual direct attention toward areas where meaningful action is possible.
