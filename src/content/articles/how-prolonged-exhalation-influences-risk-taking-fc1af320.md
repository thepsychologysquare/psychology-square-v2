<h2>The Physiological Link Between Breath Timing and Choice</h2>

<p>When cognitive scientists examine human choice under uncertainty, they typically focus on neural architecture: how the prefrontal cortex balances potential gains against potential losses, or how dopamine signaling biases us toward immediate rewards. Yet our choices do not occur in a disembodied computer. Every choice is processed inside a biological system with a beating heart, fluctuating blood pressure, and a continuous respiratory cycle. Recent work from the German Institute of Human Nutrition Potsdam-Rehbruecke and Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin demonstrates that altering the basic cadence of this system—specifically through extended exhales—directly alters our willingness to take risks. Understanding how prolonged exhalation decision making operates reveals a direct mechanical bridge connecting autonomic physiology to economic choice.</p>
